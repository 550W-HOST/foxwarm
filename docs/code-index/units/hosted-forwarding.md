## D-hosted-request-routing

The runtimes host has separate public and mesh listeners. The public `Host` selects a tenant through the [hostname directory](./hosted-runtimes-host.md#d-hosted-runtimes-host). An unknown hostname gets a bare 404; an unavailable tenant gets a bare 503. Tenant authentication, policy and application routes run at the owner. The edge never interprets a tenant credential. The receiver resolves the original authority as well, so a signed tenant and request host must agree.

`RouteDirectory.ReadAsync` joins tenant state, the unexpired lease and its boot's mesh address. Waiters can share the same in-progress read but cancel independently; these reads use the application pool and never borrow a lease control connection. A live owner wins regardless of its rank. When there is no owner, the public edge tries the [placement candidates](./hosted-runtimes-host.md#d-hosted-placement) in order. A candidate only activates locally. A candidate with a full hot-owner byte budget declines new work; this does not displace an existing owner. Each candidate attempt lasts at most a renewal interval and the remainder of the original hold. The edge rereads the lease after a decline, a competing acquisition or a remote activation. Every dispatch enters `TenantActivations.TryAdmitExact`: the same tenant, receiver boot and epoch must match a published generation whose admission gate accepts the work. The admission lasts for the handler, including the lifetime of a stream or an upgraded socket.

A wake-up or recovery scanner calls `TenantRouter.MayStartAutonomousAsync(slug, graceElapsed, token)` before a local claim. The scan excludes tenants for which no eligible role is live before applying its row limit. The router checks for a live owner and local drain/capacity, then ranks eligible boots once: rank one before grace, any eligible local boot afterward. The scanner passes the original cause to the same `TenantActivations.ActivateAsync` used by on-demand requests. The winning generation reads its persisted wake-up row itself.

## D-hosted-mesh-authentication

The mesh uses HTTP/2 on its own `mesh.port`; `FOXWARM_ADVERTISE_ADDRESS` is its advertised host and port. `FOXWARM_MESH_SECRET_FILE` supplies a base64-encoded 32-byte shared key distinct from tenant credentials and encryption keys. This is hop authentication over a trusted cluster network, not encryption or body integrity. The public listener strips `Forwarded`, `X-Forwarded-*`, `Foxwarm-Mesh` and `Foxwarm-Mesh-*` before routing. Mesh-control headers are removed before tenant dispatch.

`MeshAuthentication` signs a fresh attempt with HMAC-SHA256 over length-prefixed fields: version, purpose (activate or dispatch), sender and receiver boot, tenant, epoch (empty for activate), timestamp, a 128-bit random nonce, method, original authority, and the raw target including query. Each boot accepts timestamps within 30 seconds of its clock and atomically remembers consumed nonces across the 60-second acceptance window. A retransmission of one attempt cannot enter twice. The owner signs a pre-dispatch refusal against the particular attempt; application status codes and rejection of an already-consumed nonce are never retry evidence.

## D-hosted-forwarding-outcomes

One edge deadline covers lookup, candidate attempts, queue and the wait for a mesh connection. An attempt uses YARP's direct `IHttpForwarder` and a boot-owned `HttpMessageInvoker`. Its handler uses no proxy, redirects, cookie container or decompression. On the selected connection the request-header encoding callback marks the boundary where bytes may be written. The edge reserves a hold permit before lookup and a separate permit before retaining upload bytes. Uploads stream on their first attempt. A refused upload can be repeated if untouched or completely retained within 64 KiB; a partially consumed or larger body cannot. The response's lifetime is independent of the hold deadline from then on; a signed refusal can resume routing only within the original deadline. Once the request may have reached a tenant, no method is replayed, including GET, HEAD and OPTIONS. Without a downstream response, an ambiguous failure produces 502 with an unknown-outcome body. A response already started ends as an incomplete stream.

## D-hosted-websocket-shutdown

The upgraded protocol is WebSocket. YARP relays negotiated frames, including the final data and the Close frame with status and reason, in either direction. A client Close can receive its acknowledgement. When a transport ends, the bridge finishes the other direction after delivering what the ending peer already sent; a loss without a Close frame is abnormal. The bridge never reconnects or replays frames. On the HTTP/2 mesh, a reset affects its stream; adjacent requests keep their connection. Arbitrary TCP half-close behavior is outside this contract.

## Tests

`TenantActivationsTests` pins a published generation, checks earlier and later epochs and a different boot, and verifies that a latched gate admits nothing. `MeshAuthenticationTests` checks the signed fields, time window, per-attempt refusal proof and concurrent consumption. `TenantReplicaTests` runs Kestrel on two public fronts and two mesh listeners; `ReplicaProcessTests` exercises the production entry, real leases and process failover.

`ForwardingProcessTests` runs the supported entry point with either managed Main or `FOXWARM_TEST_MAIN_EXECUTABLE`. It checks that tenant credentials are scoped by host, an HTTP/1 chunked Session creation keeps its requested ID across the HTTP/2 mesh, and a committed bodyless creation whose reply is lost creates exactly one Session. A real CLI Node sees the owner’s 1008 and switches from its invalid credential to pairing.
